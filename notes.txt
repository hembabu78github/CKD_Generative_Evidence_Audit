1. MIMIC-IV Demo — my first choice










MIMIC-IV Clinical Database Demo

This is a freely accessible subset of 100 de-identified hospital patients. It includes real clinical observations and hospital data, unlike our synthetic trajectories. 
PhysioNet

Download MIMIC-IV Demo

Why it matters: We could evaluate repeated creatinine measurements, observation timestamps, missing values and temporal ordering using real EHR records, where the necessary fields are available.

It is an ICU/hospital cohort, not a representative outpatient CKD cohort. We would need to determine how many patients have usable kidney-related measurements.

There is also an open FHIR-formatted demo with 100 patients, published in August 2025:

2. NHANES — best larger real-world dataset










NHANES 2021–2023

CDC provides downloadable files covering serum creatinine, urine albumin and creatinine, blood pressure, diabetes and demographic characteristics. 
Continuous NHANES+1

Download NHANES laboratory files

For our experiments, download these four files first:
- DEMO_L — demographics and survey weights.
- BIOPRO_L — standard biochemical profile, including creatinine.
- ALB_CR_L — urine albumin and creatinine.
- BPXO_L — blood pressure measurements.
These files can be joined using the participant identifier SEQN.

NHANES is useful for validating baseline kidney-function distributions, missingness, calculations and subgroup behavior. However, it is generally cross-sectional rather than a patient-level longitudinal EHR.
3. UCI Chronic Kidney Disease — quick external validation










UCI Chronic Kidney Disease

Contains 400 observations and 24 features, including creatinine, blood urea, hemoglobin, blood pressure and clinical conditions. It has a CC BY 4.0 license. 
UCI Machine Learning Repository

Download UCI CKD data

This is a simple way to add an independently sourced CKD dataset for missing-value, input-consistency and robustness tests.

It is not suitable for longitudinal delay experiments because it lacks dated repeated patient observations.
4. UCI Risk Factor Prediction of CKD










Risk Factor Prediction of Chronic Kidney Disease

Contains 200 records and 28 features. It is freely downloadable under CC BY 4.0. 
UCI Machine Learning Repository

Download risk-factor dataset

This gives us another independently sourced collection, although it would add relatively little beyond Tawam and NHANES.
5. Synthea — for realistic longitudinal EHR testing










Synthea

Synthea generates longitudinal patient histories with encounters, laboratory observations, medications and diagnoses. It supports CSV and HL7 FHIR exports. 
synthetichealth.github.io

Download Synthea

This is not another real-patient dataset, but it has an advantage over our current synthetic test cases: the records are generated through a published, independent simulation framework.
